# ADR-024 · 2026-04-30 · Vendor upstream system prompts verbatim (Apache-2.0)

**Decision.** The `system-prompt` crate ships **verbatim copies** of two upstream system prompts as embedded `&'static str` constants:

- `CODEX_BASE_INSTRUCTIONS` — `openai/codex/codex-rs/protocol/src/prompts/base_instructions/default.md`
- `ZED_SYSTEM_PROMPT_TEMPLATE` — `zed-industries/zed/crates/agent/src/templates/system_prompt.hbs`

Both files live under `spikes/system-prompt/prompts/` with a `README.md` documenting source URL, license (Apache-2.0 in both cases), and last-sync date.

**Context.** Hivecore needs *good* default system-prompt text for the canonical layered builder. Writing one from scratch invents-not-borrows; the OpenAI Codex team and the Zed team have both spent significant cycles refining their text. Their prompts are open-source under Apache-2.0 — the same license we ship under.

Vendoring verbatim (rather than paraphrasing) preserves attribution clarity, makes upstream syncs trivial (diff our copy against the upstream HEAD), and avoids the risk of accidentally mutating semantics during a "rewrite for our voice" pass.

**Alternatives considered.**
- **Paraphrase / write our own.** Loses the cumulative authoring effort. Risk of subtle behavioural drift. Rejected.
- **Reference upstream by URL only.** Forces a network fetch at runtime or build time. Brittle. Rejected.
- **Pull in upstream as a Cargo dep.** No published crate ships these prompts as data. The codex repo ships them as files inside an opinionated binary; coupling to that crate's full surface is too much. Rejected.

**Consequences.**
- License compliance: `prompts/README.md` cites both sources + their Apache-2.0 license. New source files in our tree don't need per-file headers (root LICENSE files cover them), but **vendored upstream files are different** — they ship under Apache-2.0 even though hivecore is dual-licensed.
- Sync hygiene: a `scripts/sync-prompts.sh` (TODO) should diff our copy against upstream HEAD on a cadence and either auto-update or flag drift.
- The Codex prompt mentions OpenAI's specific tools (apply_patch, update_plan, etc.) by name. When hivecore reuses it, the model may behave as if those tools exist. Layer 3 must either provide compatible tools or override the base-instructions source.

**Revisit when.** A material divergence between hivecore's tool surface and Codex's emerges; or upstream relicenses; or a third reference prompt (Anthropic-published, etc.) becomes available.
